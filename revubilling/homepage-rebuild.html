Six things on the homepage, and what to do about each.

Written for the doctor who wants the money and the office manager who wants the phone to stop ringing — the two people who actually decide. Each section below: what's on the page now, what's wrong with it, and the exact words to use instead.

What this covers

Sections 1 to 4 are from screenshots of the live page. Section 5 is from the draft in the working doc. Section 6 is a block that isn't on the page at all yet.

Below the services section I don't have screenshots — testimonials, the EHR logo strip, any FAQ, and the footer. Send those and I'll write them up the same way.

The rundown

Section by section

01

Top Navigation Bar

Change
What's wrong
  • "Contact Us" and "Request A Quote" go to the same place. Two links competing for the same click means neither gets it. The call to drop one is right.
  • There's no phone number anywhere. Office managers pick up the phone before they fill in a form. That traffic currently has nowhere to go.
  • "Request A Quote" promises something you can't give in one click. You can't price a practice before you've talked to them about their specialty and their volume.
The bigger problem this points at

The page currently asks for four different things: "Request A Quote" in the nav, "Schedule A Demo" in the hero, "Speak With An Expert" right next to it, and now "Schedule a Consultation" in the new draft. A visitor shouldn't have to work out which one is the real one. Pick one and use the same words everywhere. This costs nothing and is the change most likely to bring in more calls.

What to do
  • Remove "Contact Us." Put the phone number in that spot.
  • Change the button to match the hero button — "Schedule a Consultation."
  • On phones, make the number a tap-to-call bar that stays at the bottom of the screen.

02

Hero Section

Change
What's there now
HEADLINE Stop Losing Money Every Year to Billing Errors, Denials, and Administrative Waste UNDER IT Your team saves lives. Ours saves your bottom line. We handle the billing headaches, chase down the denials, and turn your revenue cycle into a reliable one. [ Schedule A Demo ] SPEAK WITH AN EXPERT VIDEO: "Why do Healthcare Providers Choose Revu Billing"
What's wrong
  • The headline is all bad news and no relief. It tells a doctor they're bleeding money and stops there. "Administrative waste" is a phrase from a consulting report — nobody says it out loud. It's also the most common opening line in this entire industry, so it doesn't set you apart from anyone.
  • "Your team saves lives" doesn't fit most of your clients. A dentist, a chiropractor, an ABA therapist, a mental health practice — that's four of the five specialties you list, and none of them describe their work that way. The line flatters the smallest part of your market and quietly leaves out the rest.
  • "Schedule A Demo" is the wrong button. There's nothing to demo. You're a service staffed by people, not software. Someone clicks expecting a screen share and gets a sales call — and it asks for time on their calendar before they've been given a reason to give it.
  • The video sits in the best spot on the page and it's about you. "Why Healthcare Providers Choose Revu Billing" is a company video in the place a proof point should be.
What to do
  • The headline in the working doc is the right call. It talks about the reader's day instead of their losses, and "you and your team" reaches the doctor and the office manager in one line. Two small edits: drop "All Day" — nothing is lost and it fits better on a phone — and cut the sub-line from 52 words to about 35.
  • Take "97+% accuracy" out of the sub-line. You already have the real, specific version further down the page: 97% clean claim rate. A vague number sitting near a precise one makes both look soft. Use the real one, in the metrics band, where it's labelled.
  • Add a small line under the button saying what the call actually costs in time. That removes the main reason people don't click.
  • Move the video below the services section, or swap it for a client saying what changed at their practice.

03

Proof & Metrics

Change
Start with the good news

This is the strongest section on the page. The heading — "Here's What Happens When Billing Actually Works" — is genuinely good and already live. Leave it exactly as it is. (The version typed into the working doc has a typo, "Happend", and switches it to past tense. Don't use that one.)

What's wrong
  • It's in the wrong place. Someone who scrolls once and leaves never sees any of it. Three of these numbers belong in a thin line directly under the hero.
  • Six numbers is too many. Three land. Six blur into each other and none of them stick.
  • Two of them don't mean anything to a practice. "300K+ Verification of Benefits" measures how much work you've done, not what they get. "200+ Insurance Networks" — a practice bills maybe a dozen. "42 States" only matters to someone operating in more than one.
  • The labels are in your language, not theirs. A doctor does not know what a "clean claim rate" is. Say what it means and the number gets three times stronger.
Before this goes live

Every one of these numbers needs a source and a date on file — $68M+ especially. If a prospect asks where it came from, someone needs an answer ready. Anything that can't be backed up should come off the page. In this industry a number you can't support does more damage than no number at all.

Use this — three numbers, relabelled 
$68M+     Recovered for the practices we bill for
97%       Claims accepted on the first try
50+       Specialties we bill for

Put these three directly under the hero as one thin line.
Keep the full set further down if you want it, but lead with these.
04

Core Services

Change
What's wrong
  • Every card is named after the process. Six industry terms in a row, and not one of them tells the reader what actually changes at their practice. The doctor has to translate before they can care, so they don't.
  • "Turning your billing operation into a strategic advantage" is a line from a consulting deck, not something a practice owner would say.
Worth catching before it ships

The rewrite in the working doc goes further into technical language, not away from it — "converting billing operations into a measurable, strategic asset," "posted with line-item precision to preserve A/R accuracy and reporting integrity," "root-cause denial analysis," "driving measurable denial-rate reduction over time."

That version is well written, and it would land with a hospital CFO or a procurement team. But it's the opposite direction from doctors and office managers, which is the whole point of this pass. Flagging it now because it's much easier to catch here than after it's live.

What to do
  • Keep all six services — they're the right six. Just rename each card by what the practice gets, and put the industry term in a small line underneath.
  • Nobody loses anything. The doctor reads the headline. The office manager reads the sentence. Google and anyone comparing vendors still find the right terms in the small line. That's how the site sounds less technical without giving up the words you need.
  • Keep the "VIEW ALL SERVICES" link where it is.

  • The heading is written as a search-engine question. On a homepage it reads like an FAQ entry and costs a beat of attention.

Two sections disagree with each other

The metrics band says "50+ Specialties Served." This section lists five. Anyone who reads both will notice. Either the five are meant as broad categories — in which case say so — or one of the two numbers needs fixing. Worth a decision before either goes live.

What to do
  • Keep the five chips exactly as they are and make each one a link to its own specialty page. That does more for search than the question-heading was doing.
  • Replace the heading and shorten the paragraph. "A solo chiropractor to a multi-site surgical group" is something a reader can picture; "small, mid and large" isn't.

The section that's missing

Add
What's wrong

Right now the page goes: here's your problem, in general terms → here are our numbers → here's our service list. There's never a moment where a visitor thinks "that's my office." Nothing on the page describes their actual day.

This is the fix for the wrong-pain-point note. It's also how you talk to the doctor and the office manager at the same time without building two different websites — each one reads their own column and skips the other.

What to do
  • Add a short two-column block between the hero and the services section.
  • Every line is a specific thing that happens in a real practice — not a category. That's the whole reason it works.
  • On phones the columns stack, but keep both role labels.

Where to start

These three first

All three are text edits. No new design, no new photos, nothing to wait on.

FIRST Pick one call to action

The page asks for four different things today. Choose "Schedule a Consultation" and use those exact words in the nav, the hero, and the footer. Costs nothing, most likely to bring in calls.

SECOND Move three numbers up

$68M+, 97%, and 50+ go in a thin line under the hero, relabelled in plain English. Your best material is currently below the point where most people stop reading.

THIRD Rename the six service cards

Result on top, industry term underneath. This is the one change that makes the site stop sounding like it was written for other billing companies.

One more thing

The competitor everyone in dental gets compared to

eAssist's homepage headline: "Most dental practices leave money on the table. With eAssist's billing pros and AI-Powered Copilot, you'll collect more, reduce A/R headaches, and see lasting growth in as little as 60 days." Their button says "Book a consultation."

What to take: they name a timeframe — "in as little as 60 days." A specific, checkable promise beats an adjective every time. And their button asks for a conversation, not a demo or a quote.

What not to take: they lead with an AI product. Your advantage is the opposite — a real person who picks up the phone. "Talk to a Specialist, Not a Bot" is a direct shot at the category leader, and it's the best line in the working doc. Use it.
