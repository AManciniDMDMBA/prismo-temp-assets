Written for the doctor who wants the money and the office manager who wants the phone to stop ringing — the two people who actually decide. For each section: what's on the page now, what's wrong with it, and the exact words to use instead.

Five cards, identical text, identical icon, all reading "Trusted by 500+ Physicians." Either placeholder content that shipped or a section nobody filled in.

To a visitor both of these say the same thing: nobody finished this website. That's the worst possible signal from a company asking to handle a practice's money — and it undercuts every other improvement in this document.

Why this matters more than the copy

This audience checks. An office manager comparing three billing companies will notice the same page claiming 97% in one place and 99% in another. The moment one number looks shaky, all of them do — including the true ones. $68M+ recovered is a strong, real number and it deserves to be believed.

What to do: one person owns a single list of approved numbers, each with a source and a date. Every number on the site comes off that list, and nothing goes on the page that isn't on it. Also drop "Up to" — a reader hears "up to 99%" as "not 99%."

While you're at it — the company has three names

"Revu Billing" in the hero video and the contact copy, "RevuBilling" throughout the working draft, and "Dental Revu" inside one of the live testimonials. Pick one spelling and make it consistent everywhere, including the logo lockup.
