Why this matters more than the copy

This audience checks. An office manager comparing three billing companies will notice that the same page claims 97% in one place and 99% in another. The moment one number looks shaky, all of them do — including the true ones. $68M+ recovered is a strong, real number and it deserves to be believed.

What to do: one person owns a single list of approved numbers, each with a source and a date. Every number on the site comes off that list, and nothing goes on the page that isn't on it. Also drop "Up to" — a reader hears "up to 99%" as "not 99%."

One pattern worth naming

The rewrite keeps making the copy more technical, not less

Three sections now show it. The services cards become "converting billing operations into a measurable, strategic asset" and "posted with line-item precision to preserve A/R accuracy and reporting integrity." The revenue visibility section loses its short bullets for "identify aging receivables and prioritize recovery opportunities." The workflow becomes "Our Framework For Optimization."

All of it is well written, and all of it would land with a hospital CFO or a procurement team. But the brief was doctors and office managers, and this moves away from them — in a couple of places the copy already on the live site is the plainer version.

A quick test for any line before it ships: would an office manager say this sentence out loud to a colleague? If not, it needs rewriting, however professional it reads.
