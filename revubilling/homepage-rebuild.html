01 — Diagnosis

Four problems, in the order they cost you money

These aren't style notes. Each one is a specific reason a qualified visitor leaves without calling.

PROBLEM 01

The call to action promises software you don't sell

"Schedule A Demo" is the highest-friction, least-honest button on the page. RevuBilling is a service staffed by people — there is nothing to demo. A doctor clicks it expecting a screen-share of a dashboard, and either bounces when they realize it's a sales call, or arrives on the call with the wrong expectation. It also asks for a calendar commitment before the visitor has been given a single reason to spend one.

PROBLEM 02

It names the wrong pain

"Bottom line" and "revenue cycle" describe a category, not a bad Tuesday. The pain that actually moves this buyer is concrete and physical: forty minutes on hold with a payer, an appeal that's been resubmitted three times, an A/R report nobody has opened in a month, and a billing person whose resignation would take the whole process with her. The current copy never says any of that out loud, so nobody reading it feels seen.

PROBLEM 03

It's written in RCM vocabulary

Charge entry, ERA processing, days in A/R, HCPCS, clean-claim scrubbing, credentialing. The office manager knows about half of these. The doctor knows almost none and doesn't want to. The fix isn't to delete the terminology — it's proof you know the work — it's to lead every block with the plain-English outcome and keep the terminology in a caption underneath.

PROBLEM 04

The credibility is real but buried

Two decades in the business, 400+ practices, HIPAA compliance, certified coders, Google reviews. That is a strong hand played face-down at the bottom of the page. It should be the first thing under the headline, in the visitor's first eight seconds — and it should carry names, roles, and specialties, not anonymous five-star quotes.

RewriteWhere switching anxiety gets killed
Now

The onboarding story is told in process language — "short discovery," "structured onboarding and quality process," "standardize workflows," "secure access." It describes what your operation does. It does not answer the three questions actually stopping the sale: do I have to change software, what happens to my current biller, and how long is this going to take?

No demo, no slide deck. 2 A free look at your last 90 days We review your claims and A/R and show you where money is getting lost — before you sign anything. 3 We start, inside the system you already use Most practices are fully live in 2–3 weeks. [VERIFY timeline] CLOSING LINE: You don't switch software. You don't let anyone go. Your team stops doing the insurance work — and starts doing the patient work.
  • Step 2 is the offer that replaces "Schedule a Demo." It gives value before it asks for a commitment, which is why it converts a doctor who isn't shopping yet.

04 — Benchmark

What the competition does that you don't

One direct competitor, verified; plus the pattern across the category.

eAssist — dentalbilling.com

Their hero, live: "Most dental practices leave money on the table. With eAssist's billing pros and AI-Powered Copilot, you'll collect more, reduce A/R headaches, and see lasting growth in as little as 60 days." Their CTA is "Book a consultation", and their brand promise is "collect 100% of what's rightfully owed."

Three things to take: they open on the reader's loss, not on their own service. They attach a timeframe to the outcome ("in as little as 60 days") — a specific, checkable promise beats an adjective. And their primary CTA is a consultation, not a demo, for exactly the reason yours shouldn't be.

One thing not to take: they lead with an AI product. Your differentiator is the opposite — a named human who picks up the phone. "Talk to a specialist, not a bot" is a direct counter-position to the category leader. Lean into it.

Category pattern

"Schedule a demo" is the most-used CTA in B2B and one of the weakest: it asks the visitor to spend calendar time before they've been given value, and it's a poor fit for a service business with nothing to show on a screen. The current direction across the category is toward lower-friction, value-first offers — a review, an audit, a checkup. Your Step 2 ("a free look at your last 90 days") is that offer. It should be the thing the buttons are selling.

07 — Checklist

Nothing ships until all of this is true

  • The word "demo" appears nowhere on the homepage, including button labels, form headings, and the thank-you page.
  • Every statistic on the page has a documented source and a stated measurement period.
  • One CTA wording is used in the nav, hero, mid-page, and footer — identical text in all four.
  • A phone number is visible in the header on desktop and reachable in one tap on mobile.
  • Every testimonial carries a name, role, specialty, and city, with written permission on file.
  • No headshot, logo, or quote placeholder is still on the page.
  • The page renders without horizontal scroll at 375px, and the "Sound familiar?" columns stack with their role labels intact.
  • Every industry term appears only in a caption line, never in a heading or a first sentence.
  • Compliance has approved every HIPAA, BAA, and data-security statement.
  • Analytics baseline is recorded and CTA events fire correctly in a live test.
  • Page title and meta description match the new positioning.
  • All five specialty chips link to live pages.
